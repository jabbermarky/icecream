# Design: Closing the Ingredient Onboarding Gap

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-08-11

## Problem Statement

Time-to-first-churn on a *new* recipe is measured in days. That time is spent on
research, not on formulation: identifying what goes into the flavor, reading
other recipes online to understand ratios, and then determining nutritional
values for ingredients not already in the library.

Ice Ed addresses only the last step of that sequence. Balancing to targets — PAC,
POD, fat, MSNF — is solved and is the app's core competence. Everything upstream
of balancing happens in browser tabs and on paper.

Stated plainly: **Ice Ed solves the last twenty minutes of a multi-day process
and none of the days in front of it.**

A second gap was named — improving on the previous batch of a recipe — but it
has a working workaround (see Status Quo) and costs convenience rather than days.
It is scoped out of this design and revisited under Dependencies.

## Demand Evidence

- **"I already keep notes on every batch that I make, and have been for > 1
  year."** Sustained manual behavior over a year, unprompted, with no tooling
  support. This is the strongest signal in the session: a workaround someone has
  maintained by hand for twelve months is a workaround that is load-bearing.
- **"Historically, time-to-first-churn took me days"** — the only quantified cost
  discussed. Nothing else in the session has a number attached to it.
- **The gap was self-identified seven months before this session.**
  `.planning/todos/pending/2026-01-14-versioned-recipes-in-library.md` was filed
  2026-01-14 and is still pending. Three related AI todos were filed 2026-01-15.
  The problem has been legible to the founder since January; the effort has gone
  elsewhere (the Sprinkles rewrite) in the interim.
- **Existing partial investment.** The USDA FoodData Central integration in
  `js/features/ingredients.js` was built to attack exactly this problem, and it
  goes deep — it derives PAC and POD from the individual sugar breakdown. Someone
  cared enough to write real domain math against it.

**Honest limitation: N=1.** All demand evidence here comes from a single user who
is also the developer. There is no evidence in this session that other people
experience this pain, and the Sprinkles decisions doc assumes a multi-user
product without any such evidence in hand. That gap is not resolved by this
design and should not be papered over: this is a design for a tool that is
known-valuable to exactly one person.

## Status Quo

The current workflow for a new recipe:

1. Flavor profile research — read multiple ice cream recipes on cooking sites,
   subreddits and blogs; reconcile them into a rough ingredient list and ratios.
   Entirely outside the app.
2. Ingredient research — for anything not already in the library, find
   nutritional values. The app's USDA import handles part of this
   (`ingredients.js:635`), but see Premises P2: it routinely declines to
   populate PAC and POD, the two values formulation actually needs, and those get
   computed by hand in the PAC/POD calculator tool.
3. Balance in Ice Ed. This part works.
4. Churn, then record the outcome in handwritten batch notes kept outside the
   app entirely.

For iterating on an existing recipe: consult the handwritten notes, adjust the
formulation manually, churn again. The notes and the recipe are never linked;
correlation happens in the founder's head.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** an experienced formulator maintaining a curated personal
ingredient library, who already understands PAC/POD/MSNF and does not need the
concepts explained — someone for whom the bottleneck is data acquisition, not
knowledge. Today that is a population of one.

**Narrowest wedge:** *not* "build ingredient onboarding." A code read during this
session established that ingredient onboarding largely exists already. The wedge
is closing three specific gaps in the onboarding that ships today (G1, G2, B1
below). This is materially smaller than what was proposed at the start of Phase
4, and the reduction is the main product of the session.

What already works in `js/features/ingredients.js:635` (`onDownloadIngredientData`):
searches FDC across Foundation / Survey (FNDDS) / SR Legacy; retries with
truncated queries on zero hits; fuzzy-matches by Damerau-Levenshtein and presents
a pick-list; prefers Foundation over SR Legacy over Survey; derives Water, Fat,
Sugar, kcal; derives PAC and POD from the sugar breakdown against the `Sugars`
reference table; derives Solids and MSNF; reports unpopulated fields; routes
through `importIngredients` for diff-and-confirm rather than silent overwrite.

## Constraints

- **Solo maintainer.** Anything requiring ongoing operational attention is
  expensive out of proportion to its size.
- **The app is in real use.** The existing curated `data/ingredients.json` is
  years of accumulated work and must not be disturbed. Any schema change must be
  additive and backward-compatible.
- **No backend today.** The current app is static; the USDA call goes directly
  from the browser. Anything requiring a server-side proxy (e.g. an LLM key)
  crosses a line the current architecture does not cross.
- **Vanilla ES6 modules, Playwright tests.** Work lands in the existing app, not
  in the Sprinkles rewrite.
- **An in-flight rewrite plan exists** (`.planning/sprinkles-stack-decisions.md`)
  that would replace this codebase. Work here must be justifiable even if the
  rewrite proceeds — see Dependencies.

## Premises

- **P1 — The days are dominated by research, not balancing.** *Confidence: high.*
  Stated directly by the founder and consistent with the app's feature coverage.
- **P2 — Manual PAC/POD entry is a major share of ingredient research time.**
  *Confidence: high. **VERIFIED 2026-08-11** against the live FDC API.* The
  derivation gate passes for **0 of 11** representative ice cream ingredients
  under the current code. It has never fired. The cause is **not** what this
  document originally claimed — see "Correction" below.
- **P3 — FDC covers most ingredients actually being added.** *Confidence:
  medium-high. **Partially verified 2026-08-11.*** SR Legacy records carry rich
  sugar data: 10 of 11 report `Total Sugars`, and 8 of 11 carry an individual
  breakdown that reconciles against it. Foundation records carry none at all.
  Coverage is better than feared, which weakens the case for the G3 LLM
  fallback. Still unmeasured: how many ingredients actually being added are
  absent from FDC entirely.
- **P4 — A clearly labelled estimated value is more useful than a blank field.**
  *Confidence: medium.* The opposite is defensible: a blank forces attention, an
  estimate can be ignored. Mitigated by requiring estimates to be visually
  distinct at point of *use*, not only at import.
- **P5 — Review-before-commit is a feature, not friction.** *Confidence: high.*
  A wrong fat percentage entering the library unchallenged corrupts formulations
  silently for months.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Ingredient onboarding — close the gaps in what exists
Fix the three defects in the existing USDA import: graded PAC/POD derivation
(G1), per-field provenance (G2), and the fuzzy-match sort bug (B1).
*Completeness 7/10. ~1 week human / ~half a day CC.*
Attacks the blocker present inside every new recipe. Mechanical enough that no
AI judgment is involved and nothing can be silently wrong in a way review won't
catch. Builds on code that already exists and already encodes the domain math.
Does not touch flavor research, so part of the multi-day cost survives.

### Approach B: Full recipe importer from web sources
Build `.planning/todos/pending/2026-01-15-ai-recipe-importer-from-web.md`: paste
a URL or raw text, LLM parses it, converts volume to weight, fuzzy-matches the
library, researches missing ingredients, shows per-field confidence, user
approves into a draft.
*Completeness 9/10 on the gap, high variance. ~3 weeks human / ~1-2 days CC.*
The single biggest possible cut to time-to-first-churn. But it strictly subsumes
Approach A — if the ingredient half is unreliable the whole feature is
untrustworthy — and its hardest problem is calibrating when to say "I am not
sure." Requires an LLM key, therefore a backend, therefore crosses the
no-server constraint.

### Approach C: Flavor research chat
An LLM chat grounded in the ingredient library and current targets: "what goes
into a Sicilian pistachio gelato and at what ratios."
*Completeness 6/10. ~2 weeks human / ~1 day CC.*
Hits the half of the gap the founder named *first*, and the half that is slow
because it requires judgment rather than lookup. But it is the least verifiable
output in the whole space — plausible nonsense is indistinguishable from good
advice until after a churn — and it also requires the backend.

### Approach D: Close the other gap instead — link a year of batch notes to recipe versions
Import the existing handwritten notes, anchor each to the recipe version that
produced it.
*Completeness 8/10 on gap 1. ~1 week human / ~half a day CC.*
There is over a year of real data sitting outside the app, and it is the corpus
any future "improve this recipe" feature would need. But gap 1 already has a
workaround that has held for a year — this buys convenience, not days — and it
leaves the expensive gap untouched.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A**, with scope revised downward from what was originally proposed.

Rationale:

1. **It targets the one place a small fix plausibly removes hours.** G1 is a
   five-line defect sitting between the user and the two numbers the app exists
   to compute.
2. **It requires no backend**, so it does not force the API-proxy decision that
   B and C both drag in.
3. **It is a strict prerequisite for B.** The importer's hardest sub-problem is
   trustworthy ingredient values with confidence semantics. Building A first
   gives B somewhere honest to put its output.
4. **It forces `createIngredient` to exist as a domain concept**, with
   provenance, before `recipe-domain` (D13) is extracted — see Dependencies.
5. **A and D are close in cost, and A addresses the gap with a number attached.**

### Scope

**In:**
- **G1 — make the derivation fire.** Two parts, in order of impact.

  **G1a — fix the nutrient name constants.** This is the whole ballgame. The
  code asks FDC for names it no longer returns:

  | Code asks for | FDC returns | Site |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `"Glucose (dextrose)"` | `"Glucose"` | `:769` |
  | `"Sugars, Total NLEA"` / `"Sugars, total including NLEA"` | `"Total Sugars"` | `:763` |

  `getNutritionValue` returns `-1.0` on no match (`:743`), and `valid &= ...`
  (`:780`) ANDs across all seven sugar keys, so one unmatched name suppresses
  both PAC and POD (`:784-789`). The total-sugars mismatch means **`Sugar`
  itself has never populated either** — `setValue` skips any negative value
  (`:753-759`). Two string constants take the gate from 0/11 to 6/11.

  **G1b — reconcile the breakdown against the total.** For records where some
  individual sugars are absent, sum the ones present and compare to
  `Total Sugars`:

  - Sum reconciles within tolerance → the absent sugars are genuinely zero.
    Derive and mark **measured**.
  - Sum falls short of the total → the shortfall is unattributed. Apportion it
    under a declared assumption and mark **estimated**.
  - No `Total Sugars` and an incomplete breakdown → decline, and say why.

  This supersedes the hardcoded safely-zero sugar list proposed during review.
  Measured against the live API it is both simpler and more accurate: honey
  reconciles to within 0.03 g despite reporting no lactose, because honey
  contains no lactose. A hardcoded list would have called that estimated. The
  data validates itself; no chemistry table needs maintaining.

  **Measured outcome across 11 representative ingredients:** 8 measured,
  2 estimated, 1 undeliverable — against 0 today.
- **G2 — per-field provenance.** Record source (`usda:foundation`,
  `usda:sr-legacy`, `derived`, `estimated`, `manual`, later `llm`), FDC ID where
  applicable, and date. Additive sidecar; unannotated entries read as `manual`.
  Not "done" until provenance is visible in the ingredient editor.
- **B1 — sort comparator.** `distances.sort()` (`:673`) sorts lexicographically,
  so the `minDistance` threshold (`:674`) is miscalibrated and the filter
  (`:675-678`) keeps and discards the wrong candidates. Fix:
  `.sort((a, b) => a - b)`. Independently testable; worth doing regardless.

**Out, deliberately:** G3 (LLM fallback for ingredients FDC lacks) — depends on
G1/G2 and pulls in the backend question. Approach B in full. Flavor-profile
research. Anything in the Sprinkles rewrite.

**Non-goal:** full automation. See P5.

**Flagged, not in scope:** `js/features/ingredients.js:650` carries a hardcoded
USDA API key in a client-side request. It is in version control *and* exposed to
every user of the app regardless. Low severity — FDC keys are free and
rate-limited, not billable — but it should be rotated and moved to config.

## Open Questions

1. **Roughly what fraction of the ingredients added in the past year did FDC
   actually have?** *Partially answered 2026-08-11:* for common ice cream
   ingredients FDC coverage is good and SR Legacy sugar data is rich. Still open
   is the specialty end — pistachio paste, glucose syrups by DE, commercial
   stabilizer blends — which is where G3 would earn its place.
2. **When a record has `Total Sugars` but no breakdown at all, what does the
   apportioned remainder get attributed to?** *Narrowed 2026-08-11:* this is now
   known to affect 2 of 11 reference ingredients (cocoa powder, vanilla
   extract), and the reconciliation rule means it no longer fires for records
   that merely omit a sugar which is genuinely zero. Sucrose is the obvious
   default and is wrong for some foods; declining and prompting for manual entry
   is the alternative.
3. **Should re-importing an existing ingredient auto-upgrade estimated values to
   measured, or always require review?**

## Success Criteria

- **Primary:** the share of USDA imports that populate PAC and POD without
  manual entry rises from its current baseline to >80%. *Baseline is currently
  unmeasured — establishing it is part of The Assignment.*
- Every PAC/POD value carries a visible measured-vs-estimated marker at point of
  use in a formulation, not only at import.
- Existing `data/ingredients.json` loads unchanged with no migration step.
- The fuzzy-match fix is covered by a test that fails against the current
  lexicographic sort.
- **Qualitative:** on the next new recipe, ingredient research is no longer the
  step that dominates elapsed time.

1. **G1 is two nutrient-name constants, plus a reconciliation rule** (Issue 1,

## Correction — measured against the live API, 2026-08-11

Network egress to `api.nal.usda.gov` was opened after the review completed. The
premise the review rested on could then be measured instead of inferred. **The
review's diagnosis was wrong on the mechanism, right on the consequence.**

**What the review claimed:** the gate fails because it requires galactose, which
USDA rarely reports.

**What the data shows:** galactose is present in 6 of 7 records first probed and
in every SR Legacy record with a sugar profile. The gate fails because of two
nutrient-name mismatches — the code asks FDC for `"Glucose (dextrose)"` and
`"Sugars, Total NLEA"`, and FDC returns `"Glucose"` and `"Total Sugars"`. Both
lookups return `-1.0` and fail silently.

**Consequence, unchanged and now proven:** the gate passes for **0 of 11**
representative ingredients under current code. The derivation has never fired.
Also newly discovered: because the total-sugars name never matches either,
`Sugar` has not been populating on import at all — a field nobody had noticed
was broken.

**A third finding the review missed entirely.** Foundation records carry no
sugar breakdown and no `Total Sugars`:

```
Cheese, ricotta, whole milk [Foundation]
   OK       Water, Total lipid (fat), Energy
   MISSING  Sucrose, Glucose, Fructose, Lactose, Maltose, Galactose, Total Sugars
```

The code prefers Foundation over SR Legacy over Survey (`:714-735`), so for
formulation purposes it deliberately selects the record that **cannot** produce
PAC/POD over the one that can. Foundation has better sampling metadata; SR
Legacy has the sugar chemistry. The preference should depend on whether the
record actually carries sugar data.

**Measured gate results, 11 representative ice cream ingredients:**

| | current code | + corrected names | + reconciliation rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Derives, marked measured | 0 | 6 | **8** |
| Derives, marked estimated | 0 | 0 | 2 |
| Cannot derive | 11 | 5 | 1 |

The single undeliverable case is nonfat dry milk, which reports no individual
sugars and no total. It should decline and say so.

**What this changes about the plan:** T2 shrinks from a chemistry rule to two
string constants plus a reconciliation check. The hardcoded safely-zero sugar
list agreed during review is dropped as unnecessary. A new task (T9) covers the
Foundation preference. P2 moves from unverified to verified; P3 improves.
