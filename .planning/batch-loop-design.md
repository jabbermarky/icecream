## Problem Statement

A recipe in Ice Ed is a formula. What happens when you actually churn it lives
somewhere else entirely: a notebook, kept by hand, for over a year. The two are
never linked, and the correlation between "what I made" and "how it turned out"
happens only in the founder's head.

This is the second of the two gaps named in the original office-hours session.
It was scoped out of the ingredient onboarding design on the reasoning that it
"has a working workaround" and "costs convenience rather than days."

**That reasoning is now questionable.** A workaround maintained by hand for
twelve months is not evidence that the workaround is adequate. The original
design doc said as much about a different signal, then applied the opposite
logic here.

## Demand Evidence

**N=1, stated plainly.** Asked what evidence exists that anyone *other than the
founder* wants this, the honest answer was: nobody has asked. No forum request,
no friend, no shop owner. The year of handwritten batch notes is real, sustained,
unprompted demand evidence — for exactly one person.

The founder committed to looking for proof independently. That commitment needs
an address and a definition, or it becomes the eleventh untouched todo (see
Risks).

**The competitive absence, and its inversion.** A spike across six ice cream
formulation tools (Gelato-Pro, GelatoDemy, Ice Cream Calculator, Ice Cream Calc,
Free Gelato Balancing App, Scoop Science) found that all of them focus on
balancing and component analysis. None support:

- a production or batch log
- feedback on how a recipe turned out
- any mechanism for improving a recipe from its results
- **any process component at all** — a recipe is an ingredient list, full stop

Read one way, that is white space. Read the other way: several of these products
are mature and charge money, and not one of them built this. That is either a
gap they all missed or a verdict from their users. **Which it is remains
untested**, and the Q1 answer above means we currently have no evidence either
way.

## Status Quo

For a new recipe: formulate in Ice Ed, churn, then record the outcome in
handwritten notes kept entirely outside the app.

For iterating on an existing recipe: consult the handwritten notes, adjust the
formulation manually, churn again. The notes and the recipe are never linked.

Nothing in the codebase models any of this. A grep for batch, production log,
churn, overrun, aging or pasteurisation across `js/` finds exactly one hit: an
overrun *slider* at `js/app.js:135`, which is an input to a calculation, not a
record of anything that happened.

## The Versioning Substrate

`.planning/todos/pending/2026-01-14-versioned-recipes-in-library.md`, filed
2026-01-14 and untouched since, is not a competing item. It is the floor this
design stands on.

It already contains the hardest architectural thinking in this space:

- **Recipe history** — the library stores one version per name; saving
  overwrites with no history.
- **Ingredient dependency versioning** — a recipe is a formula designed against
  specific ingredient properties. If Milk's fat moves 3.0% → 3.5% and quantities
  stay fixed, the resulting ice cream is not the one that was designed.
- **Lock versus float**, via an npm analogy: `package.json` references by name
  and floats; `package-lock.json` freezes exact versions. "Make it again" wants
  lock. "Improve it" wants float, plus a diff of what changed.

**Why this gates the batch loop:** a batch record must attach to a *stable*
formula. Without versioning, "batch 3 was too icy" is unattributable — the recipe
may have silently changed underneath it. Any batch-loop design that skips
versioning is recording observations against a moving target.

```
  VERSIONING (the floor)              THE BATCH LOOP (this design)
  "which formula was this,     -->    "what happened when I made it,
   exactly?"                           and what should change?"
```

## Premises

- **P1 — A batch record is worthless without a stable formula to attach it to.**
  *Confidence: high.* Follows directly from the lock/float analysis above.
- **P2 — Structured capture survives contact with a real kitchen.** *Confidence:
  LOW, and this is the premise the whole design rests on.* The founder has
  filled a notebook for a year, which proves the habit exists on paper. It does
  not prove that structured fields get filled in at 11pm after churning, with
  cold hands, when free text is available. **Untested.**
- **P3 — The competitive absence is a gap, not a verdict.** *Confidence:
  unverified.* See Demand Evidence.
- **P4 — Process variables belong in the record, not just the formula.** *Confidence:
  medium-high.* Aging time, pasteurisation temperature, churn time, overrun and
  hardening all affect the outcome, and no competing tool models any of them. The
  founder's own notes are the evidence for which ones matter, and nobody has read
  them yet.

## Approaches Considered

**No approach has been selected. This design is incomplete until one is.**

### Approach A: Full loop — versioned recipes plus structured batch records
Recipe versions, structured outcome capture, cross-batch comparison, and
improvement suggestions derived from the history.
*Completeness 10/10. ~2 weeks human / ~4 hrs CC.*
Delivers the actual vision and builds on the lock/float thinking already worked
out. But it bets the entire build on P2, which is untested, and designs the
outcome schema from imagination while a year of real examples sits unread.

### Approach B: Minimal batch record only
One note per churn, attached to a frozen recipe snapshot. Mostly free text, two
or three structured fields.
*Completeness 5/10. ~2-3 days human / ~1 hr CC.*
Lowest friction, closest to the notebook that has already worked for a year, and
could ship in time to use on the next batch. But free text cannot drive any
improvement suggestion, so the loop half of the batch loop stays manual — and it
may be thin enough to change nothing about how iteration actually happens.

### Approach C: Notes-first, then minimal capture
Structure the existing year of handwritten notes. Derive the schema from what is
actually in them. Then build capture for exactly those fields.
*Completeness 8/10. ~1 evening transcription, then ~2 days human / ~40 min CC
once the schema is known.*
Turns a year of handwritten data into the app's first real dataset, and derives
the schema from evidence rather than imagination. It also tests P2 honestly: if
structuring a year of notes is unbearable, daily capture will be worse, and that
is worth knowing before building for it. The cost is a tedious evening with no
code in it.

**Recommendation: C, then B.** A year of real notes is the only genuine evidence
this design has, and nobody has read them. Everything else in this document is
inference.

## Open Questions

1. **Which approach?** Blocking. Nothing else can be finalised first.
2. **Who is the target user?** Unanswered. Serious home and competition makers,
   small artisan shops, culinary students, or explicitly nobody-but-the-founder
   are genuinely different products. A shop loses money on a bad batch and cannot
   train staff without a record; a home maker is merely disappointed. The
   consequence differs by an order of magnitude and so does the design.
3. **What counts as proof, and where will it be looked for?** The commitment to
   find demand evidence has no definition and no address.
4. **What process variables does the founder actually record?** Answerable in one
   evening by reading the notebook. Currently unknown, and P4 depends on it.

## Risks

**The January pattern.** Ten todos were filed on 14-15 January 2026 — AI recipe
importer, LLM recipe research, LLM troubleshooting, welcome page, UI
modernisation, mobile UX, toast notifications, library upgrade, versioned
recipes, scale-button validation. **None have been done in seven months.** This
design targets the same problem space as one of them and depends on another. The
observed failure mode in this repository is not bad planning; it is plans that
never get executed. "I will work on it on the side" is the exact phrasing that
precedes that outcome.

**Attention is elsewhere.** `.planning/sprinkles-stack-decisions.md` was edited
9 times in the last 30 days; the ingredient design doc, 6. The rewrite is the
most actively-thought-about document in the repository, and this design targets
the codebase the rewrite would replace.

## The Assignment

**Read your own notebook and count.** One evening, no code.

For the last 20 batches, record: which recipe, what you changed from last time,
what you observed about the result, and whether you could reconstruct the exact
formula you used from what is written down.

That produces four things this design cannot get any other way:

1. The **real schema** — which fields you actually record, versus which ones we
   would have invented.
2. A test of **P2** — if structuring twenty entries is intolerable, structured
   daily capture is dead and Approach B is the answer.
3. The **process variables** that matter to you, which is the thing no competitor
   models.
4. An answer to whether the notes are even **reconstructable** — if you cannot
   tell which formula produced batch 12, that is the strongest possible argument
   for the versioning substrate.

Separately, and smaller: post once in r/icecreamery or a gelato Discord asking
whether people keep batch notes and what they record. A dozen answers moves Q1
from "nobody has asked" to actual evidence, in either direction. Give it a date.

on the understanding that this lands next.

**T5 (provenance) and this design should share one vocabulary.** Provenance
records where an ingredient value came from and how much to trust it. A batch
record does the same for an observation. If this design invents a second
confidence or source concept, that is a DRY violation across the two features.
