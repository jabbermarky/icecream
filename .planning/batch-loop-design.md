## Problem Statement

A recipe in Ice Ed is a formula. What happened when it was churned lives on
paper, in a binder, and the app has never seen any of it.

This is the second of the two gaps named in the original office-hours session.
It was scoped out of the ingredient onboarding design because it "has a working
workaround" and "costs convenience rather than days."

The workaround is better than that dismissal suggested, and better than the
first draft of this document claimed. It is not a pile of loose notes. It is a
functioning manual system, and the design has to start by respecting it.

## Status Quo — the real one

```
  name with a version           "Mango V2.1"
        |
   balance in Ice Ed
        |
      PRINT                     <-- this is the snapshot. print is immutable.
        |
      churn
        |
  annotate the printed page     <-- capture surface: paper, sticky hands,
   + record the churn date          no laptop, no login
        |
   file in a 3-ring binder      <-- archive, organized by base recipe
   organized by base recipe         (all mango versions together)
        |
   next version = copy the      <-- "Mango V2.2", tweak the copy
   last one and tweak it
```

**The printed page does three jobs at once, and does them well:** it is an
immutable snapshot of the formula, the capture surface during churning, and the
archive record. Version identity is carried in the recipe name. Lineage is
carried by physical adjacency in the binder.

This is why the first draft of this document was wrong to claim the correlation
between formula and outcome "happens only in the founder's head." It happens on
the page.

## What is actually missing

Four gaps, none of which is "you cannot link notes to a formula."

1. **Version numbers are a naming convention, not data.** The app treats
   "Mango V2.1" as an opaque string. It does not know V2.2 descends from V2.1,
   so there is no lineage, no history view, and no way to ask what came before.
2. **The diff is never recorded.** "Copy the last version and tweak it" is the
   single most information-rich moment in the whole loop — it is the hypothesis.
   It exists only as the difference between two printed pages, recoverable by
   eye.
3. **The binder answers one query well and everything else badly.** "Show me all
   mango" works. "Show me every batch I called icy" or "what did I change the
   time it got better" does not.
4. **Nothing flows back.** Starting V2.2, the app cannot tell you that V2.1 was
   icy or what you did about it.

## Target User

**Serious home and competition makers who already keep notes.** People making
ice cream at home seriously enough to version their recipes and keep records.
The founder is one of them, which means design intuitions can be trusted and a
polite answer can be told from a real one.

Explicitly not: artisan shops. A shop needs the record legible to staff who were
not there, plus yield, cost per litre and food-safety logging. That is a
different product and the founder has never run production.

## Demand Evidence

**N=1, stated plainly.** Asked what evidence exists that anyone other than the
founder wants this, the answer was that nobody has asked. No forum request, no
friend, no shop owner. The year of annotated printouts is real, sustained,
unprompted demand evidence for exactly one person. The founder committed to
seeking outside evidence independently.

**The competitive absence, and its inversion.** A spike across six formulation
tools (Gelato-Pro, GelatoDemy, Ice Cream Calculator, Ice Cream Calc, Free Gelato
Balancing App, Scoop Science) found all of them focused on balancing and
component analysis, with:

- no production or batch log
- no recipe feedback mechanism
- no recipe improvement path
- **no process component at all** — a recipe is an ingredient list

Read one way that is white space. Read the other way, several of these products
are mature and charge money and not one built this, which may be a verdict
rather than a gap. **Untested.** With demand at N=1 there is currently no
evidence either way, and the design should not assume the favourable reading.

Also from the spike: ~390 ingredients, categorised by type (nuts, fats and oils,
dairy, sweeteners) in a way that probably feeds their balancing. Ice Ed has 71
ingredients and no category field. Recorded in the ingredient carryover todo.

## Premises

- **P1 — Paper wins at capture, and should keep winning.** *Confidence: high.*
  A printed page works with sticky hands, near a machine, with no login and no
  laptop. Any design that moves capture into the app at churn time is competing
  with paper where paper is strongest. This premise is the reason for the chosen
  approach.
- **P2 — The valuable thing the app can add is lineage and diff, not capture.**
  *Confidence: high.* The formula and its version history are structured data
  the app is well suited to own. The observation is prose written in a kitchen.
- **P3 — The desk step is the risk.** *Confidence: low, and this is the crux.*
  Getting outcomes back into the app requires the founder, later, at a computer,
  to attach a photo and type a few fields. Nothing about the current workflow
  requires that, and it is the step most likely to be skipped. If it is skipped,
  the app has nicer printouts and no queryable history.
- **P4 — Process variables belong in the record.** *Confidence: medium-high,
  pending the binder read.* Aging time, pasteurisation, churn time, draw
  temperature, overrun and hardening plausibly explain more bad batches than the
  formula does, and no competitor models any of them. **The binder is the
  evidence for which ones actually get recorded, and it has not been read yet.**

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Full digital loop
Versioned recipes, structured outcome capture in-app, cross-batch comparison,
improvement suggestions.
*Completeness 10/10. ~2 weeks human / ~4 hrs CC.*
The only approach that makes "show me every batch I called icy" possible. But it
re-implements version tracking that already works via a naming convention and a
printer, and its capture step competes with paper at the moment paper wins.

### Approach B: Minimal digital batch record — DROPPED
One note per churn, in the app, attached to a recipe snapshot. Dropped during the
session: strictly worse than the printed page it would replace.

### Approach C: Notes-first — CHOSEN, first
Read the binder. Twenty batches. Extract what is actually annotated.
*Completeness 8/10. One evening, no code.*
The binder is semi-structured already — versioned names, churn dates,
annotations on the formula page, filed by base recipe. It is a real dataset and
nobody has read it. It is the only evidence in this design that is not inference.

### Approach D: Print-first loop — CHOSEN, second
The app owns formula, version lineage and the diff between versions, and prints
a churn sheet built for annotation: formula, version, what changed from the
parent, and prompts for the things actually recorded. Paper keeps capture.
Later, at a desk, a photo of the annotated page is attached and three or four
structured fields are typed.
*Completeness 7/10 alone, 9/10 following C. ~1 week human / ~2 hrs CC.*
Requires zero behaviour change in the kitchen, which removes the largest risk in
the design. Makes "copy the last version and tweak" a real operation with a
recorded diff.

## Recommended Approach

**C, then D.**

Read the binder first, because the churn sheet is the entire product surface and
it cannot be designed well without knowing what goes on the page today. Then
build print-first.

Rationale:

1. **It adds capability without asking for behaviour change.** The only new habit
   is at a desk, not at the machine.
2. **It attacks the gaps that are real** — lineage as data, and the unrecorded
   diff — rather than rebuilding the snapshot mechanism that print already
   provides.
3. **It improves an artifact already in daily use.** A better churn sheet gets
   used on the next batch by definition.
4. **The evidence step is cheap and doubles as a demand test.** If a year of
   annotations turns out too sparse to act on, that is worth learning before
   building anything.

### Scope

**In:**
- **Version lineage as data.** The app learns that V2.2 descends from V2.1.
  Parent pointer, not just a name.
- **Copy-and-tweak as a first-class operation**, recording the diff. This is the
  hypothesis of each batch and it is currently lost.
- **The churn sheet.** A print layout designed for annotation: formula, version,
  diff from parent, churn date field, and prompts derived from the binder read.
- **Desk-side capture.** Photo attachment plus a small number of structured
  fields, whose names come from the binder read, not from imagination.

**Out, deliberately:** in-kitchen digital capture (competes with paper);
improvement suggestions (needs outcome data that does not exist yet); anything
multi-user; anything for shops.

**Non-goal:** replacing the binder. The binder works.

## Open Questions

1. **What does the binder actually contain?** Answerable in one evening. Gates
   the churn sheet design and P4. This is The Assignment.
2. **Will the desk step happen?** P3. Untestable until the churn sheet exists and
   a few batches have gone through it.
3. **Does anyone else do this?** Demand is N=1. The founder committed to finding
   out; it needs a date and a definition.
4. **Photo, transcription, or both?** A photo is zero-friction and preserves the
   artifact but is not queryable. Structured fields are queryable but cost
   effort. The binder read should indicate how few fields would carry most of
   the value.

## Success Criteria

- The next new version of a recipe is created by copy-and-tweak **in the app**,
  and the app records what changed.
- A printed churn sheet is used at the machine, and it is preferred to the
  current printout.
- After five batches, the app can answer "what did I change between V2.1 and
  V2.4, and what did I write about each."
- **Honest failure signal:** if after five batches the desk step has been skipped
  three or more times, P3 is disproven and the design should stop at better
  printouts rather than pretending a database is coming.

## Risks

**The January pattern.** Ten todos were filed 14-15 January 2026. None have been
done in seven months. This design targets the same space as
`2026-01-14-versioned-recipes-in-library.md` and depends on its thinking. The
observed failure mode in this repository is not bad planning, it is plans that
are never executed.

**Attention is elsewhere.** `.planning/sprinkles-stack-decisions.md` was edited 9
times in the last 30 days; the ingredient design doc, 6. The rewrite is the most
actively-considered document here, and this design targets the codebase the
rewrite would replace. Version lineage and the diff model are portable to a
rewrite; a print layout is less so.

## Dependencies

- **`2026-01-14-versioned-recipes-in-library.md`** contains the lock/float
  analysis and the npm analogy (`package.json` floats, `package-lock.json`
  freezes). Print already gives lock semantics on paper. This design needs the
  same idea in data, for the copy-and-tweak operation.
- **T5 provenance, from the ingredient design, should share a vocabulary with

- **The durable ingredient tasks are not superseded.** See

## The Assignment

**Read your binder. Twenty batches. One evening, no code.**

For each, record:

1. Which recipe and version.
2. What you changed from the previous version, and whether you can tell from the
   page alone.
3. Every process variable you wrote down — times, temperatures, overrun, aging,
   anything about the machine or the day.
4. The exact words you used about the result. Not a paraphrase. The actual words.
5. Whether you could reconstruct the formula from that page alone today.

That produces four things this design cannot get any other way: the real schema
for the churn sheet, the process variables that matter to you (which no
competitor models), your actual outcome vocabulary, and a test of whether the
paper record is as complete as it feels.

**Separately, smaller, and with a date on it:** post once in r/icecreamery or a
gelato Discord asking whether people version their recipes and what they record
about a batch. A dozen answers moves demand from "nobody has asked" to real
evidence in one direction or the other. Without a date this becomes the eleventh
January todo.
