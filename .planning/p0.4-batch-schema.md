Most of the 13 were then answered by the 2026-08-15 maintainer session, which

## What the binder settles

The audit's headline is a coverage number, and the whole schema follows from it:

> **14 of 29 churned batches produced no recorded result. 15 of 29 — 52 % —
> have a verdict. The formula side is 100 % complete.**

Three things about those 14 constrain the design more than any field list:

1. **They are churned batches, not drafts.** Confirmed with the maintainer.
   Fourteen evenings of work whose outcome is gone.
2. **Silence carries no information in either direction.** Asked whether a blank
   meant "fine and unremarkable" or "the evening ended," the answer was *a mix of
   both, can't tell now.* So the blanks cannot be mined as weak positives and
   cannot be counted as losses. They are holes.
3. **The pen was there.** Banana Cream Pie v2B carries two ink corrections to
   ingredient amounts and no result. The maintainer was at the bench, writing, and
   the result still did not get recorded.

Point 3 kills the obvious explanation. This is not "he was too busy to write" —
it is that the sheet asked for the formula and never asked for the outcome. The
audit's §2.6 proves it independently: across three print generations in two
years, every field the app gained was a planning or diagnostic field. **The
outcome side never gained a single structured field.**

**And this is now demonstrated rather than argued.** The olive-oil sheet
pre-printed a draw-temperature prompt and it came back filled, against 2 of 29
in the binder. Prompts get answered. The 14-of-29 blank rate is a property of the
sheet, not of the maintainer.

**Design consequence, and it is the whole design:** the record's job is to
*ask*. A record created only when there is something to say reproduces the
binder exactly.

## Decisions

Decisions 15–24 were taken 2026-08-14 from the audit. Decisions 25–29 were taken
2026-08-15 with the maintainer and are recorded in the decision log; they are
restated here because this is the document B1 builds against. Decisions 30–36 are
new in this revision and resolve the remaining GATE FAIL findings.

### Decision 15 — A batch is a churn event (UNCHANGED, and now enforceable)

Not a recipe version, not a print, not a sheet. One recipe version can be churned
many times; one churn can be printed twice; one printed sheet can be churned at
double mass (Mexican Chocolate v1: "Double Recipe," so the printed table is half
the mass actually made).

Under decision 16 this was an aspiration the store could not enforce, because a
`planned` batch was a batch that had not been churned. Under decision 25 it
becomes an **invariant**: every record in the batch store corresponds to a real
churn, because nothing is minted until one comes back.

### Decision 16 — REVERSED. Superseded by decision 25

~~The batch record is created at PRINT, in state `planned`.~~

Decision 16 required that the record *ask* rather than wait to be filled in, and
that requirement was right and is preserved. The mechanism was wrong, in three
ways — the third of which the review did not name:

1. **It contradicted decision 15** (GATE FAIL #4). A reprint reusing the
   `planned` batch loses the second churn.
2. **An unanswered print is not the same event as an unfinished batch.** A
   cancelled print job, a reference copy printed to read at the desk, and a sheet
   that went to the machine all produced the same `planned` record.
3. **`planned` has no data in it.** It carried a `RecipeId` and a snapshot
   reference and nothing else, existing only so that its absence of data could be
   noticed. That is a signal, and a signal does not need to be a first-class
   record that also syncs, tombstones, and answers for what happens when its
   recipe is deleted.

The maintainer's amendment (`p0.4-decision16-amendment.md`) proposed replacing it
with a print log plus mint-on-return, and asked to be talked out of it on one
specific point: evidence that reference copies and cancelled prints are rare
enough that the noise argument fails.

**That evidence cannot exist, and the reason is worth recording.** The binder is
a survivorship-filtered corpus — it contains 29 sheets that were *churned and
filed*. A sheet printed and never churned was thrown away, so it is not in the
binder to be counted. The corpus is structurally incapable of answering the
question, and no re-read of it will change that. The amendment therefore stands
unopposed on its own crux, and decision 25 adopts it.

### Decision 17 — "No verdict" is a value, not an absence (AMENDED)

The cheapest high-value item in the audit, and the direct fix for the 52 %.

`planned` and `not churned` are **removed** from the batch lifecycle. They were
never states of a churn; they were states of a piece of paper, and under decision
26 they are answered by a query over the event log instead. What remains are the
three states a churn can actually be in:

| `verdict_status` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `evaluated` | has marks and/or a diagnosis |
| `nothing_to_note` | **churned, came out as expected, deliberately no detail** |
| `not_evaluated` | churned, but the evening ended — no judgment was formed |

`nothing_to_note` and `not_evaluated` are the two the binder could not
distinguish, and distinguishing them is the difference between 14 weak data
points and 14 holes. They must be two taps, never one field left blank — and per
the sheet design, **two pre-printed boxes on paper**, because the ambiguity
originates on paper.

### Decision 18 — Ingredient order is not meaningful — FORMULA-SCOPED (decision 27)

The formula diff is a **keyed comparison**, not a sequence comparison. There are
no move or reorder operations over ingredients, and a reordering produces an
empty formula diff.

**The scoping is load-bearing.** Method inverts it: method order *is* meaningful
and a reorder is a real change. If decision 18 is applied one level wider than
its evidence, reorderings vanish from the diff — the same shape as the
duplicate-ingredient own-goal, where a decision was generalised past what it was
decided about.

The key is `row_id`, not the name — see decision 34.

### Decision 19 — Quantity changes are expressed in concentration, never in grams alone

Peppermint v1 → v2 is the proof. In grams the extract went **up** 55 % and
sucrose **up** 74 %, which reads as ignoring v1's note ("A Tad too strong on
Peppermint. A tad too Sweet."). In concentration both came **down** — extract
−17.5 %, sucrose −7.4 % (arithmetic re-verified). Both corrections were applied
correctly; the batch size moved at the same time and the sheet records grams.

Batch mass across the binder ranges 504 g → 1709 g, and the maintainer scales
recipes often (Mexican Chocolate "Double Recipe," Peppermint 504→947, Pistachio
v3.1→2 L). **A gram-based delta will regularly state the opposite of what was
done.** Grams may be shown; they may not be the comparison basis.

### Decision 20 — No return path for the prose (CLARIFIED by decision 36)

Roughly 6–13 irreducibly-prose statements across 29 batches — one every two or
three sheets. Parsing that back into structure is a large build serving a small,
high-variance corpus, and the parse would flatten exactly the hedges that carry
the meaning ("maybe," "a tad," "not as X as Y").

**This deletes a tier from the batch-loop design.** The three-tier return path
(identity / marks / prose) becomes two tiers plus an attachment.

### Decision 21 — No taxonomy of defects

"Coconut fat congealed and froze into hard blobs" is a novel failure mode that
no checkbox list anticipates. The open field is not a fallback for when the marks
do not fit — **it is where the diagnoses live**, and the audit's §2.5 shows
diagnoses are where version-to-version progress comes from: six of the ten
changes that actually altered the next batch came from prose, not marks.

The corollary is uncomfortable and worth stating: **the maintainer writes marks
when it worked and prose when it failed.** Pistachio v2 (the happiest batch): six
clean marks, zero prose. Coconut v1 (epic failure): one mark and a full mechanism
description. A marks-only sheet would be best at recording the batches he learns
least from.

### Decision 22 — Batches are their own store and their own sync record type (AMENDED by decision 26)

P0.3 put recipe identity *inside* the container because identity must stay
consistent with the recipe and one Drive file is the only atomic unit. **That
argument does not transfer to batches.** A batch references a recipe version; it
does not need to be written atomically with it, and there are many batches per
recipe. Putting them in the container would bloat every recipe save with history
belonging to past versions.

So batches get their own IndexedDB store and their own Drive files, keyed by
`BatchId`. The T3/T4 join and executor already establish the pattern (id-first
join, `SavedAt` clock, listing-failure abort, writes-before-deletes); a batch
join reuses the shape but is a separate instance — batches are append-mostly and
never renamed, so the rename machinery that dominates the recipe join is not
needed.

**Amendment:** decision 22 assumed the batch store was the only new record type.
Decision 26 adds a second — the event log. Sync gains **two** new record types,
not one, and they have different join shapes: batches join like recipes, events
union.

**Photos are attachments, not fields.** A batch whose photo has not synced is
still a valid batch.

### Decision 23 — The diff flags ingredient-definition drift (STRENGTHENED by decision 35)

Falls directly out of the audit's item 10. The same named ingredient computed
~12.5 % less PAC and POD on a newer sheet than an older one, because PAC/POD
became scaled by the ingredient's sugar fraction. Dextrose is the primary PAC
lever (26 g – 183 g across the binder) and PAC is the proxy for the hardness
problem that dominates the outcome notes — so the drift sat in exactly the
variable being tuned.

`buildRecipeContainer` already snapshots each ingredient's full definition into
the saved record, so **any two saved recipes carry the coefficients they were
computed with.**

### Decision 24 — Marks are a signed scale centred on "Good"

The audit's §2.3 collected every evaluative word in the binder and found the
native scale is **bipolar, not a quality ladder**:

> "A tad too sweet." "A little hard." "Not enough cherry flavor." "Could have
> more mango." "Too much vanilla." "Really strong cocoa."

A 1–5 quality scale is the wrong instrument: it would force "a tad too sweet" and
"not very sweet" onto the same low end of the same axis when they point in
opposite directions. The instrument is:

`−2 far too little · −1 slightly under · 0 right · +1 slightly over · +2 far too much`

with **`0` labelled "Good"** — the maintainer's own default positive, ~20 uses —
and a rarely-used `Perfect` flag above it. (He used "Perfect" exactly once, and
got there by crossing out "Good." The strikeout is data: his scale has a level
above Good that he uses sparingly.)

**Now demonstrated rather than predicted.** The olive-oil sheet used a 1–5 scale
and produced `bitterness 1–5: 5` — unreadable, because for an olive-oil ice cream
5 could be "pleasantly bitter, as good EVOO should be" or "far too bitter." The
page cannot say and neither can a reader in a year. That is the collapse the
audit predicted, caught in the act.

Some axes are one-sided in practice — nothing is ever "too smooth." The uniform
instrument still fits; those axes simply never take a positive value.

**Open:** the maintainer wrote **4.5** on a five-point integer scale, so he wants
finer resolution than five steps and a −2…+2 scale has fewer. Half-steps need a
decision. See "Still open."

### Decision 25 — Reprint is not a concept in this app (maintainer, 2026-08-15)

There is saving and there is printing. Print-versus-reprint is beyond the app's
semantics. **Every print is a print event; two prints are two events.** The batch
mints on return, not at print.

This removes `chain_root`, removes "reprint must name a `BatchId`," and dissolves
the decision 15/16 contradiction rather than patching it.

### Decision 26 — The print log generalises to a recipe event log, one immutable record per event (maintainer, 2026-08-15)

Event types: `saved`, `printed`, `batch_recorded`, `deleted`.

It buys three things a print log does not:

1. **A `deleted` event *is* a tombstone.** Issue #21 (deletes never propagate
   across devices) closes for free, for both record types.
2. **It supplies the version history** the binder's audit failure #5 is about.
3. **The nudge becomes a query** — *printed events with no `batch_recorded`
   after them* — rather than a `nudge_state` field to keep consistent across
   devices.

**The sync constraint is the hard part, and it dictates the shape.** The log
cannot be one file that gets appended to: sync is last-write-wins on whole
records, so two devices appending means one device's events are silently
overwritten — the exact silent-clobber class T4 exists to prevent. **One
immutable record per event, individually keyed, written once and never updated.**
LWW then has nothing to resolve and sync is a union.

This also clears the objection that killed the separate identity store in P0.3.
That store was rejected because identity must stay atomically consistent with the
recipe. **Events do not need that** — an event is a fact about the past, so a
partial sync leaves you with fewer facts, not wrong ones.

Consequence accepted: with immutable events, state changes become new events.
Dismissing a nudge is a `print_dismissed` event, not a field update.

### Decision 27 — Method is per-version and diffable; decision 18 is formula-scoped (maintainer, 2026-08-15)

Method belongs in the recipe container, versioned with the formula. Sous vide
temperature drifted 75 → 77 → 80 °C across the binder and the record never states
it; versioned method makes that a diff line.

**Row and step ids survive versioning while `RecipeId` mints.** For a diff
between V2.1 and V2.2 to be readable, unchanged rows and steps must keep their
ids across the save that mints the new version. Otherwise everything reads as
removed-and-added and the diff is noise. This is the opposite of the instinct
that a new version gets new everything.

**Method is not in P0.4.** Adding an entity to a schema under revision stops it
converging. But the sheet is designed as though method exists *now* — labelled
blank lines capture structured-enough method from the first sheet, and
`METHOD_STEP` absorbs it later. Paper is cheap to reprint; uncollected data is
gone.

### Decision 28 — The printed sheet is the primary capture surface (maintainer, 2026-08-15)

The app screen is transcription. Writing happens at the bench, on paper, with
cold hands; typing happens afterward, sometimes never. **The paper form's
ergonomics drive the schema's**, because paper is where the data is born. This
inverts P0.4's original assumption that the outcome instrument is an app screen
(task B6).

**Corollary, and it is a hard constraint on every field below: only prompt for
what is measurable at the bench.** Two olive-oil prompts came back unanswered and
both are the same kind — `Cream butterfat (calculated)` blank, `Overrun %` a
question mark. Neither can be produced standing at the machine. A prompt for a
number the operator cannot determine in the moment does not get filled; it gets a
question mark. Compute those in the app and print them, or leave them off the
bench sheet.

### Decision 29 — Grams is the only unit in the model (maintainer, 2026-08-15)

The baker's approach: metric weights for everything, including liquids.
Volumetric-to-weight conversion happens once, at import, never at display. This
supersedes the open question about external recipes being unmodeled — import is
planned and owns the conversion.

Two consequences the import design carries: countables (`8 large egg yolks`) need
per-unit masses rather than densities, and the as-sourced measure is retained so
a row converted at a wrong density can be re-derived.

This does **not** relax decision 19. Grams is the storage unit; percent of batch
mass is the *comparison* basis.

### Decision 30 — The snapshot is COPIED into the batch, never referenced (NEW)

*Resolves GATE FAIL #1, #2, #3.*

The 2026-08-14 schema had a `SnapshotRef` field and no referent. P0.7 was cited
as persisting an identified snapshot, but P0.7 is not built, and even when it is,
a reference is the wrong construct: the recipe it points at keeps being saved.

Two candidate fixes:

| | Embed in the batch | Separate immutable snapshot store |
|---|---|---|
| Referential integrity | nothing to dangle | a batch can outlive its snapshot |
| Recipe deleted | batch unaffected | orphan, needs a policy |
| Partial sync | batch arrives whole or not at all | batch can arrive without its formula |
| Duplication | one copy per churn | deduped across churns |
| New stores | none | one, plus GC/lifetime rules |

**Embed.** `SNAPSHOT_ROW` and `SNAPSHOT_INGREDIENT_DEF` hang off `BATCH_CONTAINER`
and are copies, not references — `source_row_id` records where a row came from
without depending on it. The duplication cost is a few KB per churn against a
corpus of ~29 batches in two years. The referential-integrity cost is not
bounded, and P0.3's central lesson was that **one Drive file is the only atomic
unit available**: a batch that is one file is a batch that cannot half-arrive.

This is the same trade P0.3 made for identity, decided the same way, for the same
reason.

**Where the snapshot lives between print and return.** Decision 25 mints the
batch on return, so between the two the printed formula must survive a save. The
`printed` event carries the snapshot; on return the batch copies it. The
practical consequence, which the amendment did not name: **the `printed` event is
not a lightweight signal, it carries the payload.** That is the honest cost of
mint-on-return. What it buys is worth it — the batch store's invariant stays
clean and the reference-copy case stays distinguishable — but the pre-churn
record does not disappear, it moves to the event log where it cannot be mistaken
for a churn.

**Corollary:** a sheet whose event has not synced can still be captured. The QR
carries `RecipeId` + `SchemaVersion` + `print_nonce`; scanning on a device that
has never seen the `printed` event still mints a batch bearing that nonce, and
the event reconciles later by nonce join. **The batch is authoritative; the event
log is advisory.** State that in B5 or a device-boundary case will be read as a
bug.

### Decision 31 — A batch is never deleted by its recipe (NEW)

*Resolves GATE FAIL #8 — the behaviour `batch-loop-design.md:536` says P0.4 owes
and `app.js:299` currently does not define.*

`RecipeId` on a batch is **advisory**, exactly as `parent_recipe_id` is advisory
on a recipe. Deleting a recipe emits a `deleted` event (decision 26) and does not
cascade.

The reason is the audit's, not a database instinct: a batch is a record of an
evening that happened. The recipe is a plan; the batch is history. Deleting the
plan cannot un-happen the evening, and the batch carries its own embedded
snapshot (decision 30), so it remains fully readable with the recipe gone. It
displays as an orphan with its snapshot name rather than vanishing.

`orphan_policy` on `BATCH_CONTAINER` records which rule a given batch was written
under, so the behaviour can change later without rewriting history.

### Decision 32 — `overall` is unsigned quality and leaves the signed axis set (NEW)

*Resolves GATE FAIL #11.*

Decision 24's instrument is bipolar because the binder's vocabulary is bipolar.
`overall` has no bipolar reading: "far too much overall" is not a thing anyone
wrote, and "−2 overall" and "+2 overall" would both have to mean bad, which
breaks the one property that makes the scale legible.

So `overall_rating` moves to `BATCH_CONTAINER` as a plain unsigned quality value
and is not an `OBSERVATION.axis`. The signed axes stay strictly bipolar, and the
one axis that is a ladder is stored as a ladder.

### Decision 33 — Deltas are grouped, and a delta carries a range (NEW)

*Resolves GATE FAIL #12.*

`{target, direction, from, to}` fails on two things the maintainer actually
wrote:

- **A range.** *"increase fat to 8%, 9-11% optimal"* — a target and an acceptable
  band, not a point.
- **A coupled constraint.** *"Less Dextrose, more Sucrose"* holds PAC roughly
  constant while lowering POD. As two independent deltas it reads as two
  unrelated changes and the reason is lost — which is audit failure #5 again.

So: `DELTA_GROUP` **1-to-many** `DELTA`. The group carries `constraint_note`
("keep PAC, lower POD") and the `applied` / `applied_in_recipe_id` pair; each
`DELTA` carries `from_pct_of_batch` and a `to_pct_min` / `to_pct_max` band, which
collapses to a point when the two are equal.

**`applied` is validated by the external sheets**, not invented: *"try 7"* and
*"Try with 4 TBS rum in mix"* are intents recorded before being acted on. A
proposed delta that was never applied is a real state.

**Keep `from`.** The pages that recorded "from 15g to 8g" are the only ones whose
reasoning survives being read cold a year later — and, tellingly, they are the
*typed* ones. The audit's §2.4 finding: **the moment the app gave him a field, he
filled it with a properly-formed delta including the previous value — something
he never once did by hand.**

### Decision 34 — The diff keys on `row_id`, not on the ingredient name (NEW)

*Resolves GATE FAIL #13, and it was a self-inflicted wound.*

`batch-loop-design.md:455` already recorded the exact failure: *"Ingredients are
an ordered array permitting duplicate names; a name-keyed diff collapses
duplicates."* The mitigation had two parts — diff array occurrences, **and**
decide whether order is meaningful. Decision 18 answered the second; the schema
then did precisely what the first warns against. The binder has a real instance:
Peppermint v1 carries two "Peppermint Candy" rows.

`FORMULA_ROW` gets a `row_id` primary key that **survives versioning** (decision
27) plus a `duplicate_ordinal` to disambiguate repeated names for display. The
diff keys on `row_id`. A rename then reads as a change rather than
remove-plus-add, which also retires the "known limit" the 2026-08-14 text
accepted.

**The general lesson, worth keeping:** the own-goal was a decision applied one
level wider than the evidence it was decided on. Decision 27's scoping of
decision 18 is the same defect caught before it shipped.

### Decision 35 — The diff recomputes under one coefficient set; it does not merely flag drift (NEW)

*Resolves the P2 the audit is emphatic about.*

Flagging that two snapshots used different coefficients tells the reader the
comparison is invalid without telling them what the valid comparison is. The
dextrose case — ~12.5 % less PAC and POD for an unchanged ingredient — then reads
as an unexplained warning next to numbers that are still wrong.

Every `INGREDIENT_DEF` and `SNAPSHOT_INGREDIENT_DEF` carries a
`coefficient_set_id`. When two sides differ, the diff:

1. reports the drift explicitly, **before** the formula changes, and
2. **recomputes both sides under the newer set** and shows that comparison as
   the primary one, labelled with which set it used.

The as-printed numbers remain visible as provenance. This is the difference
between "these two are not comparable" and "here is what actually changed."

**Binder import inherits this rule** and is out of scope for P0.4: when the 29
pages are imported, every historical formula must be recomputed under one current
model rather than trusting the printed totals (audit §2.6). The app's own saved
history does not need it — it carries its own coefficients.

### Decision 36 — The typed field is primary; the photo is an attachment; nothing is parsed (NEW)

*Resolves the P2 on the muddled prose contract.*

Decision 20 said "photograph it and stop," which read as *the photograph is the
storage medium* — implying OCR to get anything back, which is out of scope, and
leaving the typed open field's status unclear.

Three separable things, now separated:

| | Where it lives | What reads it |
|---|---|---|
| The diagnosis, typed | `OBSERVATION.prose` — always present, never a taxonomy | full-text search |
| The sheet as an object | `PHOTO_ATTACHMENT.image` | a human, on screen |
| Text known about the photo | `PHOTO_ATTACHMENT.full_text` | full-text search |

`full_text` is **populated by transcription, not extraction** — by the person
typing up the sheet, at whatever fidelity they choose, or left empty. No OCR is
in scope, and nothing parses either field into structure. Decision 21 stands: the
open field is where diagnoses live, not a fallback for when the marks do not fit.

A batch with a photo and no typed prose is valid and searchable only by its other
fields. That is a real and accepted loss, and it is strictly better than the
binder, where the sheet is the only copy and lives in a cupboard.

## The batch record

`p0.4-data-model.mmd` is normative. This section is the reasoning behind the
fields that are not self-evident.

### A. Identity & provenance

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| `BatchId` | minted **on return**, not at print (decision 25) |
| `RecipeId` | **advisory** link to the P0.3 identity of the recipe version. Never cascades (decision 31) |
| `SavedAt` | author-time clock, the same pattern P0.3 established. Was missing (GATE FAIL #6) |
| `print_nonce` | binds back to the `printed` event. From the sheet's QR |
| `churn_date` | **user-entered, distinct from the print timestamp.** Present on 5/29 pages; the print timestamp misleads — Mexican Chocolate v1 printed 12/12 and was made 12/13 |
| `batch_multiplier` | as-made mass ÷ snapshot mass. "Double Recipe" made the printed table half the truth |
| `verdict_status` | decision 17's three values |
| `overall_rating` | unsigned quality, off the signed axis set (decision 32) |
| `orphan_policy` | which deletion rule this batch was written under (decision 31) |

`VersionLabel` is **not** on the batch. It lives on the snapshot, as authored —
the maintainer uses at least four numbering conventions (v1, v2B, v2.1, v1.0,
v3.1, "v3.1 (2L)"), so it is a display string, never parsed for ordering. Issue
#16's parent pointer is what carries lineage, and per the sheet design it must
accept **a URL plus a retrieval date**, not only a `RecipeId`, because a third of
the binder originates outside the app.

### B. Formula — embedded snapshot, plus as-made overlay

Per decision 30 the planned formula is **copied** into the batch as
`SNAPSHOT_ROW` + `SNAPSHOT_INGREDIENT_DEF`. What the batch adds on top is what
happened at the bench, and it is one field on the row rather than a side table:

`SNAPSHOT_ROW.actual_grams` is the ink on the sheet. **Six of 29 pages carry ink
corrections to printed amounts**; on those pages the printed formula is not the
as-made formula. The olive-oil sheet adds four more (`45 seconds → 60`, milk
`~250 g → 263`, cream `252.8 → 241`, whole milk `370.4 → 383`).

**An as-made *addition* is a snapshot row with no planned amount.** Cherry Garcia
v1 is the case: a handwritten "Vanilla | 5" row outside the model, excluded from
every computed total — **and "Too much Vanilla" was the one thing recorded about
the batch.** The page records a defect caused by an ingredient its own arithmetic
does not know exists. This is the binder's only unreconstructable formula, and
the schema's job is to make it impossible: **an addition must recompute the
batch's PAC/POD/solids**, or P0.4 reproduces the audit's worst completeness loss.

### C. Process

`PROCESS_RECORD`, one per step as followed, with `method_step_id` null meaning
improvised. Ranked by how often the maintainer wrote each one *unprompted*, out
of 29 pages. Every temperature is unit-tagged — °C and °F alternate freely, and
Mexican Chocolate v1 crossed out "75C" and wrote "176F," which is 80 °C: a 5 °C
move made by accident of unit (issue #17).

| Field | Pages | Note |
|---|---|---|
| sous vide temp + duration | 17 | three values in play (75/77/80 °C), trending upward, never stated |
| prep method | 9 | **first-class, not a margin.** Cook / don't cook / boil-dehusk-roast / blanch / strain — the highest-variance and most consequential axis |
| aging duration | 9 | units wildly inconsistent: "overnight," "8 hrs," "4 hrs 15 min," "~~4 Hr~~ 2.5 hrs" |
| churn duration | 6 | but see the timeline problem below |
| blend duration / homogenize | 5 | includes the negative case: "No homogenization after Sousvide" |
| mix-in timing | 4 | `offset_minutes` — "added Mixins @ 45 minutes (too late)" |
| base temp into churn | 3 | |
| **draw temp** | **2** | see below |
| hardening duration | 4 | |
| serving temp measured | 3 | measured at the point of the complaint |
| machine | 1 | "Whynter" |
| churn speed | 1 | "Churned on Fast/Hard" |
| pre-chill bowl | 1 | |

**Draw temp and churn duration get prompted, not just offered.** The audit's
sharpest process finding:

> Hardness or scoopability appears in outcome notes on 9 pages. Draw temperature
> appears on 2. **You have been trying to solve a texture problem by moving
> formula variables while leaving the churn-side variables largely unlogged.**

The dominant complaint has no data behind it. This is no longer a prediction: the
olive-oil sheet prompted for draw temperature and got `−6`. Every other field can
be blank without comment; these two should cost a deliberate skip.

**Two known gaps in `PROCESS_RECORD`, both from the olive-oil sheet:**

1. **A struck-through step has no state.** Process step 1 — *"Lecithin into the
   oil"* — is scored through in its entirety. `method_step_id: null` covers a step
   performed but not printed; there is no inverse for a printed step deliberately
   not performed. Strikethrough is the maintainer's consistent notation for "not
   this" across all three formats, and the record of what was *rejected* is wanted
   — the same argument as no-verdict-being-a-value.
2. **Churn is a series, not a scalar.** `Speed A @ 20 minutes` · `Really thick @
   24 minutes` · `Full churn 30 minutes` — three time-stamped observations inside
   one churn, against a schema modelling `churn_duration` as one number.

Both are listed in "Still open" rather than resolved here, because both are
plausibly `METHOD_STEP` problems and decision 27 keeps method out of P0.4.

### D. Outcome

**D1. Marks** — `OBSERVATION`, decision 24's signed scale.

Axes: `sweetness · hardness · scoopability · smoothness · dryness · gumminess ·
viscosity · melt · flavor_intensity`.

- **`viscosity` is new** (GATE FAIL #9). Evidence: "Sweet cream is thick"
  (Strawberry V2), "Mixture was really thick before Churning" (Mango V3), "Really
  thick @ 24 minutes" (olive oil). Three sheets, three formats.
- **`quality_value` is separate from `signed_value`** (GATE FAIL #10). The audit
  is explicit at line 94: "flavor is good" *and* "could use more" are about the
  same ingredient on two different axes, and *"a single 1–5 field collapses them
  and loses the actionable half."* Intensity is bipolar; quality is a ladder.
- **`overall` is not here** — decision 32.
- **Axes are a default set plus recipe-specific ones.** The olive-oil sheet's
  axes were `oil character`, `bitterness`, `sweetness`; the first two are
  meaningless for Mango. `OBSERVATION.axis` is a string, so the model already
  supports this — the 2026-08-14 design did not.

Two dimensions the binder proves are needed:

- **`occasion`** — `at_draw` | `from_freezer`. Where the observation was made.
- **`subject_location`** for flavor marks — base vs mix-in. "Could have more PA in
  Sweet Cream" distinguishes pineapple *in the base* from pineapple *as a mix-in*,
  and the maintainer makes that distinction himself.

Flavor intensity is a repeated observation keyed by
`subject_ingredient_name`, not one axis: "Too much Vanilla," "A Tad too strong on
Peppermint," "could have more Mango."

One extra mark with no sensory content, written once and clearly a real decision
input: `PROCESS_RECORD.effort_verdict` — "Filtering Pineapple was too much work."

**D2. Diagnosis** — `OBSERVATION.prose`, always available, never a taxonomy
(decisions 21, 36).

**D3. Next-version delta** — `DELTA_GROUP` → `DELTA` (decision 33).

This is the highest-value writing the maintainer produces and it is currently
trapped in prose with nowhere else to go: "lower Stabilizer to 2.5g/Kg,"
"increase fat to 8%, 9-11% optimal," "Dextrose 86g →55g," "Allulose +30g,"
"vanilla 5→3," "Less Dextrose, more Sucrose."

**D4. Comparison** — `OBSERVATION.compared_to_batch_id` with an axis and a
direction.

"Not as Hard as Strawberry V2 / Not as Soft as Mocha" is how he actually
calibrates, it is the only prose pattern recurring in a regular enough shape to
structure, and it is the thing a paper sheet can never do and a database does
trivially.

## What a diff contains

A diff is computed between two **snapshots**. Batch-to-batch diffs are snapshot
diffs plus the as-made overlay.

**Formula rows are keyed by `row_id` (decision 34), not by name, and not by
position (decision 18).** Method steps are ordered and a reorder is a real change
(decision 27).

### 1. Ingredient set

- `added` — a `row_id` in B, not in A
- `removed` — a `row_id` in A, not in B
- `changed` — in both, quantity or name differs

A rename now reads as a change, not remove-plus-add. Duplicate names no longer
collapse.

### 2. Quantity changes — three bases, one of them primary

| Basis | Role |
|---|---|
| **% of batch mass** | **primary.** Decision 19 |
| PAC / POD contribution | for sugars — the levers actually being tuned |
| grams | shown for reference, never the comparison basis (decisions 19, 29) |

### 3. Spec changes

Style (Gelato / Premium / Super-Premium), serving temp, hardness %, overrun %.
Type changes are real and consequential: Banana Cream Pie went Gelato → Premium
between v1 and v2B, nearly doubling fat.

**And the type's range is checked** — not only the recipe's own stored target.
Cranberry v1.2 printed "347 PAC (220 - 230)", the largest spec violation in the
binder, with an Error row reading 0 % across every column. Both are true: the
Error row measures against whatever the recipe already was. The sheet had
everything needed to stop that batch and stopped nothing. **Issue #15.**

### 4. Computed metric changes

PAC, POD, PAC:POD, % solids / water / sugar / non-lactose sugar / fat / milk fat
/ MSNF / stabilizer, kcal, L base, L ice cream.

### 5. Coefficient drift, and recomputation (decision 35)

For every ingredient present in both snapshots, compare the stored definitions.
Any difference is reported **separately from formula changes and before them**,
and both sides are then **recomputed under the newer coefficient set** to produce
the primary comparison.

Without this, the dextrose case reads as a formula change that never happened.

### 6. Process changes

Field-by-field over block C, when both sides are batches rather than bare
snapshots.

### Not in the diff

- **Ingredient order.** Decision 18, formula-scoped. Method order **is** diffed
  (decision 27).
- **Notes / Observations text.** Not a formula property, and it carries stale
  across versions (issue #14). Diffing it would report noise.
- **Identity fields.** `RecipeId`, `SavedAt`, `BatchId` are provenance, not
  content.

## What this changes in `batch-loop-design.md`

1. **The return path loses a tier.** Three tiers (identity / marks / prose)
   becomes two plus a searchable attachment. Decisions 20, 36.
2. **P1.1's parent pointer moves earlier, out from behind the batch entity.**
   Cranberry v1.2 walks back the sugar swap v1.1 confirmed worked, and is
   ingredient-for-ingredient identical to v1.0 — it is v1.0 re-opened and
   re-printed, not a child of v1.1. Confirmed with the maintainer as a mistake,
   not a deliberate test. **Issue #16**, and it must accept a URL.
3. **The type-range check joins it. Issue #15.**
4. **Neither is a Tier 2 feature.** Both belong in the formula half already
   considered finished, and either would have caught v1.2.
5. **The outcome surface is paper first** (decision 28), which inverts B6 — it is
   a print-format task before it is a UI task.
6. **The event log is new work that decision 22 did not scope.** B5 gains a
   second record type with a different join shape.

## Implementation tasks — revised sequence

The 2026-08-14 order put the diff before storage and treated B3 as independent of
P0.7. Both were wrong. **B3 cannot precede P0.7; they are one cut.**

- [ ] **B1** — models — the batch container: `BatchId`, advisory `RecipeId`,
      embedded snapshot, `SavedAt`, `verdict_status`. Same canonical-builder +
      refusal-gate treatment as the recipe container (P0.2/P0.5 precedent).
      Decisions 15, 17, 30, 31, 32.
- [ ] **B2** — storage — batch store + `listBatchesStrict`, mirroring the recipe
      backends, plus deletion semantics. Decisions 22, 31.
- [ ] **B2.5** — the event log store. One immutable record per event, individually
      keyed. **New, from decision 26** — it did not exist in the original list.
- [ ] **P0.7 + B3** — one cut. Print persists an identified snapshot *and* emits a
      `printed` event carrying it; the batch mints on return and binds by nonce.
      Decisions 25, 26, 30.
- [ ] **B5** — sync — batch join (recipe-shaped) **and** event union (new shape).
      The batch is authoritative; the event log is advisory. Decisions 22, 26, 30.
- [ ] **P1.1** — lineage / parent pointer. Issue #16.
- [ ] **B4 + P1.2** — the diff module, pure. Keyed by `row_id`,
      concentration-based, recomputing under one coefficient set. Decisions 18,
      19, 34, 35.
- [ ] **B6** — the outcome surface. **Paper form first** (decision 28), then the
      app screen as transcription. Belongs with the redesign.

Ahead of all of it: **issues #15 and #16**, each roughly an hour, either of which
alone would have caught Cranberry v1.2.

## Still open

Carried forward, plus what this revision could not close.

1. **Half-steps on the signed scale.** The maintainer wrote 4.5 on a five-point
   scale; −2…+2 has fewer steps, not more. Needs a decision before the sheet
   prints.
2. **A struck-through process step** has no representation. Possibly a
   `METHOD_STEP` problem, which decision 27 keeps out of P0.4.
3. **The churn timeline** — a series of time-stamped observations, not a scalar.
   Same caveat.
4. **`occasion` may need a third value.** `at_draw` and `from_freezer` both
   describe finished ice cream, but Strawberry V2's only observations were about
   the *base* ("Good Flavor in Sweet Cream," "Sweet cream is thick") and Mango V3
   noted the mixture before churning. Without somewhere to put a pre-churn
   observation, either those get filed as verdicts on the finished product — which
   is what makes the audit's 15/29 wrong — or they get dropped.
5. **Which event types earn their place.** `saved` on every save may be noise.
6. **Photos portable or local-only** (decision 1). Still load-bearing: decision 36
   keeps the photograph as the medium for the sheet as an object.
7. **Payload format version, byte one.** Must be settled before the first sheet
   prints — every sheet printed against a format that later changes is
   unrecoverable. This is the one open question that accrues dead paper while it
   stays open.
8. **Duplex or two simplex pages** — binder economy versus unclip-flip at the
   bench.
9. **The dextrose sugar fraction** wants one confirmation against the live
   ingredient library. See `binder-audit-verification.md`.
10. **Binder import is out of scope for P0.4**, and inherits decision 35's
    recomputation rule when it happens.
