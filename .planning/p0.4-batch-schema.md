# P0.4 — The batch record, and what a diff contains

**Status:** DESIGNED 2026-08-14, from the binder audit. Not yet implemented.
**Evidence base:** `.planning/binder-audit.md` (29 churned batches, Aug 2024 –
Apr 2026) and `.planning/binder-audit-verification.md`. Every field below is
derived from something actually written on at least one page, or from a failure
the audit demonstrated. Nothing here is invented from theory.
**Unblocks:** P0.4 and P0.7, which were gated on the binder read.

---

## The question this answers

`batch-loop-design.md` left two decisions open and gated on reading the binder:

> **Is a reprint a new batch, or the same batch?** And is a cancelled print a
> record at all? The QR identifies a batch, but "batch" currently conflates a
> printed sheet with an actual churn.

> **Is ingredient order meaningful in the diff?**

Both are answered below. The second was answered directly by the maintainer
(decision 18); the first falls out of the audit's central finding.

---

## What the binder settles

The audit's headline is a coverage number, and the whole schema follows from it:

> **14 of 29 churned batches produced no recorded result. 15 of 29 — 52 % —
> have a verdict. The formula side is 100 % complete.**

Three things about those 14 that constrain the design more than any field list:

1. **They are churned batches, not drafts.** Confirmed with the maintainer.
   Fourteen evenings of work whose outcome is gone.
2. **Silence carries no information in either direction.** Asked whether a blank
   meant "fine and unremarkable" or "the evening ended," the answer was *a mix of
   both, can't tell now.* So the blanks cannot be mined as weak positives and
   cannot be counted as losses. They are holes.
3. **The pen was there.** Banana Cream Pie v2B carries two ink corrections to
   ingredient amounts and no result. The maintainer was at the bench, writing, and
   the result still did not get recorded.

Point 3 kills the obvious explanation. This is not "he was too busy to write" —
it is that the sheet asked for the formula and never asked for the outcome. The
audit's §2.6 proves it independently: across three print generations in two
years, every field the app gained was a planning or diagnostic field (target
ranges, composition percentages, error rows). **The outcome side never gained a
single structured field.** The result half is 52 % blank because it was never
built, not because the maintainer does not care about results — every note in the
binder is about a result.

**Design consequence, and it is the whole design:** the batch record's job is to
*ask*. A record that is created only when there is something to say will
reproduce the binder exactly.

## Decisions

### Decision 15 — A batch is a churn event

Not a recipe version, not a print, not a sheet. One recipe version can be churned
many times; one churn can be printed twice; one printed sheet can be churned at
double mass (Mexican Chocolate v1: "Double Recipe," so the printed table is half
the mass actually made).

A batch references a recipe version and carries its own identity. The QR
identifies the **batch**.

### Decision 16 — The batch record is created at PRINT, in state `planned`

This is the decision that answers "is a reprint a new batch." Rejected
alternative: create the record when an outcome is entered. That is what the
binder already does, and it produces exactly the 14 holes — a batch with no
outcome leaves no trace that it happened.

Creating at print inverts it: **every churn starts life as a record that is
visibly missing its verdict.** The 14 become a queue you can see and answer,
instead of an absence you cannot detect. P0.7 already persists an identified
snapshot before emitting the sheet; the batch record is created in the same
transaction.

- **A reprint of a snapshot that already has a `planned` batch reuses it.** It
  does not create a second. Reprinting is not churning.
- **A cancelled print is not detectable** from the browser, so it leaves a
  `planned` batch that was never churned. That is what the `not churned` state is
  for — see decision 17. It costs one tap to clear and is strictly better than
  the alternative, which is not knowing.

### Decision 17 — "No verdict" is a value, not an absence

The cheapest high-value item in the audit, and the direct fix for the 52 %.
Terminal states for a batch:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `planned` | printed, not yet resolved. The queue. |
| `evaluated` | has marks and/or a diagnosis |
| `nothing to note` | **churned, came out as expected, deliberately no detail** |
| `not evaluated` | churned, but the evening ended — no judgment was formed |
| `not churned` | printed and never made (includes cancelled prints) |

`nothing to note` and `not evaluated` are the two the binder could not
distinguish, and distinguishing them is the difference between 14 weak data
points and 14 holes. They must be two taps, never one field left blank.

### Decision 18 — Ingredient order is not meaningful (maintainer, 2026-08-14)

The diff is a **keyed comparison over ingredient names**, not a sequence
comparison. There are no move or reorder operations, and a reordering produces an
empty diff. See "What a diff contains" below.

### Decision 19 — Quantity changes are expressed in concentration, never in grams alone

Peppermint v1 → v2 is the proof. In grams the extract went **up** 55 % and
sucrose **up** 74 %, which reads as ignoring v1's note ("A Tad too strong on
Peppermint. A tad too Sweet."). In concentration both came **down** — extract
−17.5 %, sucrose −7.4 % (arithmetic re-verified). Both corrections were applied
correctly; the batch size moved at the same time and the sheet records grams.

Batch mass across the binder ranges 504 g → 1709 g, and the maintainer scales
recipes often (Mexican Chocolate "Double Recipe," Peppermint 504→947, Pistachio
v3.1→2 L). **A gram-based delta will regularly state the opposite of what was
done.** Grams may be shown; they may not be the comparison basis.

### Decision 20 — No return path for the prose. Photograph it and stop.

Roughly 6–13 irreducibly-prose statements across 29 batches — one every two or
three sheets. Parsing that back into structure is a large build serving a small,
high-variance corpus, and the parse would flatten exactly the hedges that carry
the meaning ("maybe," "a tad," "not as X as Y").

**This deletes a tier from the batch-loop design.** The three-tier return path
(identity / marks / prose) becomes two tiers plus an attachment. Full-text search
over the prose is in scope; parsing it is not.

### Decision 21 — No taxonomy of defects

"Coconut fat congealed and froze into hard blobs" is a novel failure mode that
no checkbox list anticipates. The open field is not a fallback for when the marks
do not fit — **it is where the diagnoses live**, and the audit's §2.5 shows
diagnoses are where version-to-version progress comes from: six of the ten
changes that actually altered the next batch came from prose, not marks.

The corollary is uncomfortable and worth stating: **the maintainer writes marks
when it worked and prose when it failed.** Pistachio v2 (the happiest batch): six
clean marks, zero prose. Coconut v1 (epic failure): one mark and a full mechanism
description. A marks-only sheet would be best at recording the batches he learns
least from.

### Decision 22 — Batches are their own store and their own sync record type

P0.3 put recipe identity *inside* the container because identity must stay
consistent with the recipe and one Drive file is the only atomic unit. **That
argument does not transfer to batches.** A batch references a recipe version; it
does not need to be written atomically with it, and there are many batches per
recipe. Putting them in the container would bloat every recipe save with history
that belongs to past versions.

So batches get their own IndexedDB store and their own Drive files, keyed by
`BatchId`, and sync gains a second record type. The T3/T4 join and executor
already establish the pattern (id-first join, `SavedAt` clock, listing-failure
abort, writes-before-deletes); a batch join reuses the shape but is a separate
instance — batches are append-mostly and never renamed, so the rename machinery
that dominates the recipe join is not needed.

**Photos are attachments, not fields.** A batch whose photo has not synced is
still a valid batch. This keeps decision 1 (are photos portable?) off P0.4's
critical path — see "Still open" below.

### Decision 23 — The diff flags ingredient-definition drift

Falls directly out of the audit's item 10. The same named ingredient computed
~12.5 % less PAC and POD on a newer sheet than an older one, because PAC/POD
became scaled by the ingredient's sugar fraction. Dextrose is the primary PAC
lever (26 g – 183 g across the binder) and PAC is the proxy for the hardness
problem that dominates the outcome notes — so the drift sat in exactly the
variable being tuned.

`buildRecipeContainer` already snapshots each ingredient's full definition into
the saved record, so **any two saved recipes carry the coefficients they were
computed with.** The diff must compare those definitions and report drift
explicitly. Without it, a diff between an old and a new snapshot silently
attributes a model change to a formula change.

### Decision 24 — Marks are a signed scale centred on "Good"

The audit's §2.3 collected every evaluative word in the binder and found the
native scale is **bipolar, not a quality ladder**:

> "A tad too sweet." "A little hard." "Not enough cherry flavor." "Could have
> more mango." "Too much vanilla." "Really strong cocoa."

A 1–5 quality scale is the wrong instrument: it would force "a tad too sweet" and
"not very sweet" onto the same low end of the same axis when they point in
opposite directions. The instrument is:

`−2 far too little · −1 slightly under · 0 right · +1 slightly over · +2 far too much`

with **`0` labelled "Good"** — the maintainer's own default positive, ~20 uses —
and a rarely-used `Perfect` flag above it. (He used "Perfect" exactly once, and
got there by crossing out "Good." The strikeout is data: his scale has a level
above Good that he uses sparingly.)

Some axes are one-sided in practice — nothing is ever "too smooth." The uniform
instrument still fits; those axes simply never take a positive value.

## The batch record

### A. Identity & provenance

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| `BatchId` | minted at print; the QR's payload |
| `RecipeId` | links to the P0.3 identity of the recipe version |
| `SnapshotRef` | the container as printed — P0.7 persists it. Carries the ingredient definitions, which is what makes decision 23 possible |
| `VersionLabel` | as authored. He uses at least four numbering conventions (v1, v2B, v2.1, v1.0, v3.1, "v3.1 (2L)") — this is a display string, never parsed for ordering |
| `PrintedAt` | machine-stamped |
| `ChurnedOn` | **user-entered date, distinct from `PrintedAt`.** Present on 5/29 pages; the print timestamp misleads — Mexican Chocolate v1 printed 12/12 and was made 12/13 |
| `BatchMultiplier` | as-made mass ÷ snapshot mass. "Double Recipe" made the printed table half the truth |
| `State` | decision 17's five values |

### B. Formula — by reference, plus as-made overrides

The planned formula is the snapshot; it is never copied into the batch. What the
batch adds is what happened at the bench:

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| `AsMadeOverrides[]` | `{ingredient, plannedGrams, actualGrams}`. **Six of 29 pages carry ink corrections to printed amounts** — on those pages the printed formula is not the as-made formula |
| `AsMadeAdditions[]` | `{ingredient, grams}`. Cherry Garcia v1 is the case: a handwritten "Vanilla \| 5" row outside the model, excluded from every computed total — **and "Too much Vanilla" was the one thing recorded about the batch.** The page records a defect caused by an ingredient its own arithmetic does not know exists. This is the binder's only unreconstructable formula |

An addition must recompute the batch's PAC/POD/solids, or the schema reproduces
the one failure the audit rates as its worst completeness loss.

### C. Process

Ranked by how often the maintainer wrote each one *unprompted*, out of 29 pages.
Every field is unit-tagged — °C and °F alternate freely in the binder, and
Mexican Chocolate v1 crossed out "75C" and wrote "176F," which is 80 °C: a 5 °C
move made by accident of unit.

| Field | Pages | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `SousVideTemp` + `SousVideDuration` | 17 | three values in play (75/77/80 °C), trending upward, never stated |
| `PrepMethod` | 9 | **first-class, not a margin.** Cook / don't cook / boil-dehusk-roast / blanch / strain — the highest-variance and most consequential axis, and the changes that moved results more than sugar tuning did |
| `AgingDuration` | 9 | units wildly inconsistent: "overnight," "8 hrs," "4 hrs 15 min," "~~4 Hr~~ 2.5 hrs" |
| `ChurnDuration` | 6 | |
| `BlendDuration` / `Homogenize` | 5 | includes the negative case: "No homogenization after Sousvide" |
| `MixInTiming[]` | 4 | `{what, atMinute}` — "added Mixins @ 45 minutes (too late)" |
| `BaseTempIntoChurn` | 3 | |
| **`DrawTemp`** | **2** | see below |
| `HardeningDuration` | 4 | |
| `ServingTempMeasured` | 3 | measured at the point of the complaint |
| `Machine` | 1 | "Whynter" |
| `ChurnSpeed` | 1 | "Churned on Fast/Hard" |
| `PreChillBowl` | 1 | |

**`DrawTemp` and `ChurnDuration` get prompted, not just offered.** The audit's
sharpest process finding:

> Hardness or scoopability appears in outcome notes on 9 pages. Draw temperature
> appears on 2. **You have been trying to solve a texture problem by moving
> formula variables while leaving the churn-side variables largely unlogged.**

The dominant complaint has no data behind it. Every other field can be blank
without comment; these two should cost a deliberate skip.

### D. Outcome — three sub-kinds

**D1. Marks** — decision 24's signed scale, on the nine axes the binder actually
names and nothing more:

`sweetness · hardness · scoopability · smoothness · dryness ·
gumminess/chewiness · melt · flavor intensity (per named ingredient) · overall`

Two dimensions the binder proves are needed:

- **`stage`** — `base` | `finished`. Strawberry V2 recorded only how the *base*
  tasted ("Good Flavor in Sweet Cream," "Sweet cream is thick") and never the
  finished ice cream; Mango V3 noted "Mixture was really thick before Churning."
  Without a stage dimension those count as verdicts when they are not — the
  audit's 15/29 depends on the distinction.
- **`location`** for flavor marks — base vs mix-in. "Could have more PA in Sweet
  Cream" distinguishes pineapple *in the base* from pineapple *as a mix-in*, and
  the maintainer makes that distinction himself.

Flavor intensity is a repeated field keyed by ingredient, not one axis: "Too much
Vanilla," "A Tad too strong on Peppermint," "could have more Mango."

One extra mark with no sensory content, written once and clearly a real decision
input: **`PrepEffort`** on a named step — "Filtering Pineapple was too much work."

**D2. Diagnosis** — one open text field, always present, never a taxonomy
(decision 21). Photographable and full-text searchable (decision 20).

**D3. Next-version delta** — `{target, direction, from, to}` where `target` is an
ingredient or a parameter, expressed per decision 19 in concentration or PAC/POD
contribution.

This is the highest-value writing the maintainer produces and it is currently
trapped in prose with nowhere else to go: "lower Stabilizer to 2.5g/Kg,"
"increase fat to 8%, 9-11% optimal," "Dextrose 86g →55g," "Allulose +30g,"
"vanilla 5→3," "Less Dextrose, more Sucrose."

**Include `from`.** The pages that recorded "from 15g to 8g" are the only ones
whose reasoning survives being read cold a year later — and, tellingly, they are
the *typed* ones. The audit's §2.4 finding: **the moment the app gave him a
field, he filled it with a properly-formed delta including the previous value —
something he never once did by hand.**

**D4. Comparison** — one primitive, `{axis, moreOrLess, otherBatchId}`.

"Not as Hard as Strawberry V2 / Not as Soft as Mocha" is how he actually
calibrates, it is the only prose pattern recurring in a regular enough shape to
structure, and it is the thing a paper sheet can never do and a database does
trivially.

## What a diff contains

The maintainer asked for this explicitly. A diff is computed between two
**snapshots** (a saved recipe container each). Batch-to-batch diffs are
snapshot diffs plus the as-made overlay.

**It is a keyed comparison over ingredient names — not a sequence comparison
(decision 18).** Reordering produces an empty diff. There are no move
operations.

### 1. Ingredient set

- `added` — in B, not in A
- `removed` — in A, not in B
- `changed` — in both, quantity differs

**Known limit:** ingredient identity is the name, and there are no ingredient
ids, so a rename reads as `removed` + `added`. Acceptable at N=1; worth naming
because the same class bit the recipe join (P0.3 item 18).

### 2. Quantity changes — three bases, one of them primary

| Basis | Role |
|---|---|
| **% of batch mass** | **primary.** Decision 19 |
| PAC / POD contribution | for sugars — the levers actually being tuned |
| grams | shown for reference, never the comparison basis |

### 3. Spec changes

Type (Gelato / Premium / Super-Premium), serving temp, hardness %, overrun %.
Type changes are real and consequential: Banana Cream Pie went Gelato → Premium
between v1 and v2B, nearly doubling fat.

### 4. Computed metric changes

PAC, POD, PAC:POD, % solids / water / sugar / non-lactose sugar / fat / milk fat
/ MSNF / stabilizer, kcal, L base, L ice cream.

### 5. Ingredient-definition drift (decision 23)

For every ingredient present in both snapshots, compare the stored definitions —
Sugar, Fat, MSNF, Solids, PAC, POD, Stabilizer, kcal. Any difference is reported
**separately from formula changes and before them**, because it means the two
snapshots are not on the same scale.

Without this, the dextrose case reads as a formula change that never happened.

### 6. Process changes

Field-by-field over block C, when both sides are batches rather than bare
snapshots.

### Not in the diff

- **Order.** Decision 18.
- **Notes / Observations text.** Not a formula property, and it carries stale
  across versions (see issue filed below). Diffing it would report noise.
- **Identity fields.** `RecipeId`, `SavedAt`, `BatchId` are provenance, not
  content.

## What this changes in `batch-loop-design.md`

1. **The return path loses a tier.** Three tiers (identity / marks / prose)
   becomes two plus a searchable attachment. Decision 20.
2. **P1.1's parent pointer moves earlier, out from behind the batch entity.**
   Cranberry v1.2 is the case: it walks back the sugar swap v1.1 confirmed
   worked, and is ingredient-for-ingredient identical to v1.0 — it is v1.0
   re-opened and re-printed, not a child of v1.1. The version number says
   otherwise and nothing on the page carries a parent pointer. Confirmed with the
   maintainer as a mistake, not a deliberate test.
3. **The type-range check joins it.** The same page prints "347 PAC (220 - 230)"
   — the largest spec violation in the binder — with an Error row reading 0 %
   across every column. Both are true: the Error row measures against the stored
   per-recipe target, which is whatever the recipe already was, while the type's
   range is never checked. The sheet had everything needed to stop that batch and
   stopped nothing.
4. **Neither is a Tier 2 feature.** The audit's judgment, and it is right: both
   belong in the formula half already considered finished, and either would have
   caught v1.2.
5. **Marks, the delta block and the comparison primitive are all new UI**, so
   they should be designed into the UI replacement rather than bolted onto the
   current surface — the same conclusion the redesign triage reached for P0.6.

## Still open

- **Decision 1 (are photos portable?) is now load-bearing, not deferrable
  forever.** Decision 20 makes the photograph the storage medium for the highest-
  value 20 % of the record. Decision 22 keeps it off P0.4's critical path by
  making photos attachments — a batch without its photo is still a batch — but
  the question has to be answered before the prose is trusted to the system.
- **Binder import is out of scope for P0.4.** When those 29 pages are imported,
  every historical formula must be recomputed under one current model rather than
  trusting the printed totals (audit §2.6). The app's own saved history does not
  need this — it carries its own coefficients.
- **The dextrose sugar fraction** wants one confirmation against the live
  ingredient library. See `binder-audit-verification.md`.
