# Change justification: create the batch on return, not at print

**Author:** Mark Lummus
**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Affects:** decisions 15, 16, 22 - blocker group 2 - tasks B1, B3, B5, P0.7
**Status:** ACCEPTED and SUPERSEDED, 2026-08-16. Adopted as decision 25 (reprint
is not a concept; batch mints on return) and generalised by decision 26 (the
print log becomes a recipe event log, one immutable record per event). Kept for
its reasoning; `p0.4-batch-schema.md` is now the live text.

**On §9's request to be talked out of it:** the evidence named — that reference
copies and cancelled prints are rare — cannot be produced. The binder is a
survivorship-filtered corpus of sheets that were churned *and filed*; a sheet
printed and never churned was thrown away and is not there to count. No re-read
changes that, so the proposal stood unopposed on its own crux.

**One cost §8 understated.** It says the proposal does not touch blocker group 1.
True as far as it goes, but mint-on-return makes the log the only record existing
between print and return, so the log entry must carry the printed snapshot. The
`printed` event is not a lightweight signal; it carries the payload. The
pre-churn record does not disappear, it moves somewhere it cannot be mistaken for
a churn — which is still the right trade, but it is a different one than "deletes
a state."

---

## 1. Summary

Decision 16 creates a batch record at print time in state `planned`. The outside review found that this contradicts decision 15, because a second churn of the same snapshot cannot be recorded if the reprint reuses the planned batch. The likely resolution on the page is to make reprint name a `BatchId` and become a separate command from churn.

This document proposes a different resolution. Do not create the batch at print. Create it when the results come back from the churn, and add a separate print log ("the log") that records only that a sheet was printed. The log is what drives the nudge. The batch record then only ever exists because a churn actually happened.

The change removes the contradiction rather than patching it, and it deletes a state from the batch lifecycle instead of adding a command to the print path.

## 2. Why decision 16 exists

Decision 16 was not arbitrary. It was chosen so the record would ask rather than wait to be filled in, and the evidence for that is the strongest number in the binder audit: 14 of 29 churned batches produced no recorded result, and silence is ambiguous between "it was fine" and "the evening ended." A `planned` batch sitting unanswered is a visible hole. That is the requirement, and it is the right one.

The proposal here keeps the requirement and changes the mechanism.

## 3. The problem with create-at-print

Three problems, only the first of which the review named.

An unanswered print is not the same event as an unfinished batch, and decision 16 collapses them. A cancelled print job, a reference copy printed to read at the desk, and a sheet that went to the machine all produce the same `planned` record.

A reprint has no correct behavior under decision 16. Reusing the planned batch loses the second churn (blocker group 2). Minting a new one on every reprint fills the store with records for paper that never met cream. Naming a `BatchId` at reprint works, but it adds a command to the print path to compensate for a record that should not have existed yet.

The `planned` state has no data in it. It carries a `RecipeId`, a snapshot reference and nothing else, and it exists only so that its absence of data can be noticed. That is a signal, and a signal does not need to be a first-class record in the batch store that also syncs (decision 22), tombstones, and answers for what happens when its recipe is deleted.

## 4. The proposed change

Log the print. Mint the batch on return.

A print appends an entry to the log with a `print_nonce`, the `RecipeId`, a timestamp, and an intent of either bench sheet or reference copy. Nothing else is created. The entry is unreferenced by any batch until a batch comes back and binds to it.

The nudge reads the log. An entry in state `pending` after three days produces the prompt, e.g. "You printed Olive Oil Gelato v2 on the 3rd - did you churn it?" The three answers are: results to record, which opens capture and mints the batch; churned but nothing to note, which mints a batch with `verdict_status` of `nothing_to_note` per decision 17; and not churned, which dismisses the entry.

Reprints share a `chain_root` so the log nudges once per chain rather than once per sheet.

## 5. What this preserves

The visible hole is preserved. A pending log entry asks exactly as loudly as a `planned` batch, and it asks with better information because it knows whether the sheet was a bench sheet or a reference copy.

Decision 15 is preserved without amendment, and in fact is easier to hold. A batch is a churn event. Two churns of the same snapshot are two batches, because nothing was minted in advance to be reused.

Reattachment is preserved. The Quick Response ("QR") code on the sheet carries `RecipeId`, `SchemaVersion` and `print_nonce`. On return the app scans, finds the log entry, mints the batch and binds it. Create-at-print was buying the identifier that the sheet needs to carry, and the `print_nonce` buys the same thing without minting a batch.

Decision 17 is preserved. "Nothing to note" and "not evaluated" remain distinct recorded values, and they are now reachable directly from the nudge in one tap.

## 6. Costs accepted

The log is a noisier signal than a batch record. Some fraction of nudges will be about paper that was never churned, and the intent flag reduces that without removing it.

The log needs its own answer on sync. Decision 22 syncs batches as their own record type. If the log is local-only, a sheet printed on the desktop will not nudge on the phone. This is a new question that decision 16 did not have to answer, and it should be answered explicitly rather than inherited.

A dismissed entry records less than a cleared `planned` batch would. Under decision 16, "not churned" is a value on a durable record. Under this proposal it is a dismissal on a log entry, and if the log is ever pruned that fact goes with it.

## 7. Schema deltas

Add a `PRINT_LOG_ENTRY` record with `print_nonce` as its key, plus `RecipeId`, `printed_at`, `intent`, `nudge_state` and `chain_root`.

Remove `planned` from the batch lifecycle. The batch states become churned, and whatever states the capture flow needs after that.

Remove the reprint command from the batch model. Add `print_nonce` to the batch container as the binding back to the log.

The QR payload becomes `RecipeId` + `SchemaVersion` + `print_nonce` rather than `BatchId` + `RecipeId` + `SchemaVersion`, which is what P3.1 currently specifies.

## 8. What this does not change

This proposal does not touch blocker group 1. Snapshot durability is the more serious finding and it is independent of when the batch is minted - a batch created on return still needs an immutable snapshot with a durable referent, and binding by `print_nonce` does not supply one. Group 1 should still be fixed first, because the snapshot contract determines what the batch binds to.

Groups 3 and 4 are untouched as well.

## 9. Next steps

Decisions 15, 16 and 22 should be amended together rather than 16 alone, since 22 assumes the batch store is the only new record type and this adds a second one.

B3 changes from create-at-print to bind-on-return, and its coupling to P0.7 loosens: the print path now needs to persist a log entry and an identified snapshot, but not a batch.

The sync question for the log needs an answer before B5.

I am happy to be talked out of this. The specific thing that would change my mind is evidence that the reference-copy and cancelled-print cases are rare enough that the noise argument does not hold, in which case decision 16 with a named `BatchId` at reprint is the smaller change.
